## Design Specificity Verdict

HeroPhone re-enacts the exact user journey the product sells (search "Dentista em Osasco" → bad Maps results fly away → winning profile → call in progress), and Comparison borrows real Google Business Profile visual grammar instead of an abstract chart. Pain names hyper-specific, Brazilian-small-business failure modes ("Perfil Fantasma," "Perfil Sem Dono") instead of generic SaaS pain points. ## Overall Impression

The page is well-built and genuinely product-specific where it counts (Hero, HeroPhone's narrative, Comparison), backed by a real, coherent, documented design system. But it undercuts itself in two very different ways: one is a shipping-readiness bug (every CTA points at a fake phone number), and the other is a design-system discipline gap (the single most eye-catching component, HeroPhone, is also the one place that quietly abandons the token system and drifts toward the exact generic "AI SaaS" look the project's own DESIGN.md says to avoid). Fix the number and tighten HeroPhone's palette back into the system, and this becomes a genuinely strong, specific landing page rather than a strong one with a live landmine in it.

## What's Working

1. **HeroPhone's literal narrative animation** — search → bad-profile cards fly away → winning profile → call in progress. It demonstrates the product's mechanism instead of describing it, directly satisfying DESIGN.md's "motion must have a literal referent" rule.
2. **Comparison section's Google-Business-Profile mimicry** — lets a non-technical shop owner *feel* "invisible vs. impossible-to-ignore" in one glance, doing more persuasive work than a paragraph of SEO jargon could.
3. **Restraint on social proof** — per PRODUCT.md's explicit "don't fabricate testimonials" constraint, Authority stays disciplined (positioning copy only, no fake star counts or client logos) — rare in this genre and protects credibility.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Every CTA on the live site points at a placeholder WhatsApp number.**
Why it matters: `lib/site.ts`'s `WHATSAPP_NUMBER` is still `5599999999999` (commented "substitua pelo número real"). This is the site's *only* conversion mechanism — Hero, header, and Final CTA all deep-link here. A visitor who completes the entire persuasion arc and clicks cannot actually reach the business. This isn't a polish issue, it prevents the core task outright.
Fix: Replace the placeholder with the real WhatsApp Business number before this goes live anywhere real traffic can reach it.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] No persistent conversion point through the page's longest stretch.**
Why it matters: Between Hero and Final CTA (Solution → Comparison → Authority, ~3-4 screens), the only visible WhatsApp action is the small (`size="sm"`) header button, competing visually with 4 nav links and a moving ticker above it. Comparison is this page's emotional peak (per the emotional-journey read) and has no adjacent action point — exactly where a mobile visitor (Casey) would want to act on impulse and can't.
Fix: Add a floating WhatsApp CTA (bottom-right, appearing after the visitor scrolls past Hero), reusing the existing `WhatsappButton`, or give the scrolled-header CTA more visual weight relative to the nav links.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] HeroPhone's animation loop ignores `prefers-reduced-motion`.**
Why it matters: `app/globals.css` correctly gates the CSS-class animations (marquee, float, rise) behind `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`, but `hero-phone.tsx`'s imperative `requestAnimationFrame` loop has no such check and runs its full cinematic forever regardless of the setting — and it's the largest, busiest, most sustained motion on the page, right beside the primary headline.
Fix: Check `window.matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches` in the effect and skip the loop (or render a single static end-state frame) when it's set.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] HeroPhone quietly breaks the documented design system and drifts toward the confirmed anti-reference.**
Why it matters: DESIGN.md's "One Signal Rule" says Signal Blue is the only saturated hue, and the confirmed anti-reference explicitly rejects generic "AI SaaS" visual language. The detector found 11 raw, untokenized colors (`#0d1729`, `#1f4fd8`, `#334155`, `rgba(0,38,255,.3)`, etc.) and 3 cyan-gradient/neon-on-dark hits, all concentrated in this one component's mockup chrome — exactly the palette the system was built to avoid. It reads as app-chrome simulation, which is a reasonable exception in principle, but it's currently undocumented and ungoverned, so nothing stops the next edit from letting it sprawl further.
Fix: Either fold HeroPhone's mockup colors into named DESIGN.md/sidecar tokens (so the exception is deliberate and bounded) or tighten the mockup's palette back toward Signal Blue + neutrals.
Suggested command: `/impeccable polish`

**[P3] Small consistency and semantic slips.**
Why it matters, bundled: (a) the free offer is "Diagnóstico Gratuito" in header/Hero but "Análise Gratuita do Meu Negócio" in Final CTA — same offer, different words, costs a first-timer a beat of doubt; (b) on mobile, Hero's secondary link ("Ver como funciona o método") inherits stretch alignment from its unstyled `flex-col` parent and renders full-width/centered instead of left-aligned; (c) the detector found a heading-level skip (`<h1>` directly followed by `<h3>` "Clínica Odonto" inside HeroPhone, no `<h2>` between) — a real semantic-HTML/screen-reader issue; (d) the detector found a warning-icon `<span>` 50% covered by an opaque `phone-shadow` container (`text-occlusion`) — a genuine visual clipping bug, not a false positive.
Fix: Pick one term for the free offer across all three CTAs; add `items-start` to the mobile hero-link container; promote or restructure the phone-mockup's fake heading so it doesn't skip a level; nudge the occluded icon out from under `phone-shadow`'s stacking.
Suggested command: `/impeccable polish`
